Markup vs Margin — What's the Difference?

This is one of the most common sources of confusion in business. Markup and margin both measure profitability but they're calculated differently.

Markup % = (Profit ÷ Cost) × 100
Margin % = (Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100

Example: You buy a product for $50 and sell it for $70. Your profit is $20.

• Markup = $20 ÷ $50 = 40% (40% above cost)
• Margin = $20 ÷ $70 = 28.6% (28.6% of selling price)

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is markup always higher than margin?
Because markup uses the smaller number (cost) as the base, while margin uses the larger number (selling price). The same profit amount divided by a smaller base always gives a larger percentage.
What markup should I use for my business?
It depends on your industry. Retail typically uses 50–100% markup. Food service often uses 200–300%. Software and digital products can be 1000%+. The key is covering all your costs and generating enough profit.
