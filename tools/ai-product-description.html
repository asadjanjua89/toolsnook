How to Write Product Descriptions That Convert

Most product descriptions fail because they describe the product instead of selling it. A list of features tells the customer what something is — a great description makes them feel why they need it. Here's how to write descriptions that actually drive sales.

Lead with benefits, not features

The most common mistake in product copywriting is listing features instead of communicating benefits. A feature is what a product has. A benefit is what it does for the customer.

❌ Feature-focused (weak)
"Made from 100% merino wool. 200g weight. Machine washable at 30°C."
✅ Benefit-focused (strong)
"Stay warm without the itch — our merino wool blend regulates your body temperature naturally, so you're comfortable from morning meetings to evening walks. Throws in the machine at 30°C when life gets messy."

Write for your specific customer

The best product descriptions speak directly to one person — your ideal buyer. Before writing, ask: who is buying this? What problem does it solve for them? What objection might they have? What would make them trust this product? Use the AI generator above and fill in the "target customer" field — this is the single biggest factor in description quality.

Use sensory and emotional language

People buy emotionally and justify rationally. Words that engage the senses — smell, texture, sound, weight, taste — make products feel real to someone reading a screen. Words that trigger emotions — comfort, confidence, joy, nostalgia, safety — make products feel necessary.

Address the main objection

Every product has a common customer objection. For clothing: "Will it fit?" For electronics: "Is it easy to set up?" For food: "Does it taste good?" Identifying and addressing the number one objection directly in your description removes the barrier to purchase.

How to write Amazon bullet points that rank

Amazon's A9 algorithm gives significant weight to bullet points. Best practices: start each bullet with a CAPITALISED benefit keyword (e.g. "DISHWASHER SAFE:"), keep each bullet under 200 characters, include your primary keyword in bullet 1 or 2, address a different benefit or feature in each bullet, and end bullet 5 with a risk reducer (guarantee, returns policy, or warranty).

💡 Pro tip: Always read your product description out loud before publishing. If it sounds unnatural or robotic, it will read that way to customers too. The best descriptions sound like a knowledgeable friend describing a product they love.

SEO for product descriptions

Product description SEO works differently from blog SEO. Your primary keyword should appear naturally in the first sentence. Use semantic variations throughout — not just your exact keyword phrase. Google's algorithm for product pages gives heavy weight to click-through rate and add-to-cart rate, so write for conversion first and SEO second. A description that converts well will rank well over time because Google measures user engagement signals.

How do I make product descriptions SEO-friendly?
Include your primary keyword naturally in the first sentence. Use semantic variations throughout. Avoid keyword stuffing — use each keyword once or twice.
